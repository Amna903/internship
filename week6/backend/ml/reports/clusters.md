# Clustering: real patients and a positive control

> Synthetic, educational data. Generated by `python -m ml.cluster`; do not edit by hand. Clusters are
> exploratory descriptions of engagement patterns, not categories of people, and never a medical
> assessment.

Rules, fixed before running: K-Means and agglomerative clustering (Ward) for k = 2–8; the
best silhouette chooses k, and within 0.01 of the best, fewer clusters win. Features and scaling as in
[cluster_features.md](cluster_features.md).

## 1. Real synthetic patients: is there any structure?

5,000 patients. The generator plants no patient habits, so the question is whether clustering
finds anything beyond chance.

| k | silhouette, k-means | silhouette, agglomerative (Ward) | k-means inertia (elbow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.146 | 0.112 | 12,442 |
| 3 | 0.169 ← | 0.130 ← | 10,572 |
| 4 | 0.172 | 0.120 | 9,375 |
| 5 | 0.171 | 0.120 | 8,651 |
| 6 | 0.169 | 0.105 | 8,171 |
| 7 | 0.151 | 0.086 | 7,784 |
| 8 | 0.131 | 0.063 | 7,446 |

**Baseline.** 20 reshuffles (seed 42) that move appointments between patients while each
patient keeps their number of appointments. This keeps every link that comes from how the features are
calculated (one-appointment patients still have shares of exactly 0 or 1; phone and app shares still come
from the same bookings) and removes only habits. Each reshuffle goes through the same procedure,
including choosing its own best k.

Verdict rule: structure found only if the real silhouette beats the 95th percentile of the baseline.

Reproducibility note: Ward's tree can differ between machines when patients have identical feature
values (common with about four appointments each), because exactly tied merges are ordered by
floating-point rounding. Individual baseline runs may therefore differ slightly between machines; the
verdict, the chosen k, the 95th-percentile bar and the median of the baseline runs are checked strictly.

| method | chosen k | real silhouette | baseline 95th percentile | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k-means | 3 | 0.169 | 0.173 | **no structure found** |
| agglomerative (Ward) | 3 | 0.130 | 0.163 | **no structure found** |

**No structure found.** The clusters on the real data are no better than on data where patients have no habits by construction. That matches how the data was generated: any grouping here would be splitting noise.

False-pattern checks (decisive only when structure is found):

- **k-means**: the split is mostly about `appointments` (63% of its variance lies between clusters); largest share of one-appointment patients in a cluster: 8%. Reported for completeness; with no structure found, there is nothing to call a habit.
- **agglomerative (Ward)**: the split is mostly about `appointments` (54% of its variance lies between clusters); largest share of one-appointment patients in a cluster: 8%. Reported for completeness; with no structure found, there is nothing to call a habit.

## 2. Positive control: are planted habits recovered?

1,500 fictional patients from a separate generator (`ml/cluster_control.py`, seed 7) with
4 planted engagement habits, planted with overlap. The habit labels are used only to score recovery.

| k | silhouette, k-means | silhouette, agglomerative (Ward) | k-means inertia (elbow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.276 | 0.286 ← | 3,404 |
| 3 | 0.295 ← | 0.274 | 2,527 |
| 4 | 0.274 | 0.228 | 2,115 |
| 5 | 0.253 | 0.208 | 1,869 |
| 6 | 0.237 | 0.188 | 1,715 |
| 7 | 0.225 | 0.172 | 1,583 |
| 8 | 0.221 | 0.176 | 1,479 |

Adjusted Rand index against the planted habits (1 = perfect recovery, 0 = no better than chance):

| method | chosen k | ARI at chosen k | ARI at the planted k (4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| k-means | 3 | 0.518 | 0.431 |
| agglomerative (Ward) | 2 | 0.179 | 0.459 |

**Result: the planted habits were partially recovered (best ARI 0.518).** The silhouette rule did not land on the planted 4 groups with either method; reported as-is.
K-Means stability over 10 seeds: mean pairwise ARI 0.998 (lowest 0.993).

Words used for ARI, as rough guides (there is no universal scale): 0.8 or more: recovered well; 0.5 or more: partially recovered; 0.2 or more: weakly recovered;
below 0.2: not recovered. **These thresholds were set after seeing this result.** 0.52 sits just above the 0.5 threshold, so the 'partially recovered' label is borderline.

### Diagnostic (not the result): what limits recovery?

ARI at the planted number of groups (so the first row differs from the main result, which is at the k
the silhouette rule chose), with one suspected cause removed at a time. The control and the main result
above are unchanged.

| setting | k | ARI, k-means | ARI, agglomerative (Ward) |
|---|---|---|---|
| all features, all groups | 4 | 0.431 | 0.459 |
| without the appointments feature | 4 | 0.585 | 0.642 |
| without the 'mixed' group | 3 | 0.888 | 0.937 |

At the planted number of groups, leaving out `appointments` changes the best ARI from 0.459 to 0.642; leaving out the 'mixed' group changes it to 0.937. The in-between group is the larger limit. Real data: the K-Means split there is mostly about `appointments` (63% of its variance between clusters). **Lesson: `appointments` both blurs real groups (control) and drives a false split (real data)**, because it differs between patients by chance and counts as one sixth of every distance.

## 3. What the two results show together

With the same features, scaling and methods, the planted habits were partially recovered on the control data
(best ARI 0.518), and the real data showed nothing beyond chance. So the methods
can pick up planted structure, imperfectly, and the real "no structure" result is not just a broken
pipeline. Clusters
are always exploratory: K-Means returns k groups for any data, which is why the baseline and the control
matter more than how tidy the groups look.
